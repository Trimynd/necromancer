## 6. Risks & Open Questions

*   **Unit Economics (Critical Risk):** At ₹2500/month, if providing 3 meals a day, the revenue per meal is ~₹27. Even at 1 meal a day, it is ~₹83. Both are below or at the very edge of the market floor (₹80-₹100), leaving no room for delivery or app overhead.
*   **Commodity Volatility (Risk):** Wholesale vegetable prices in Maharashtra fluctuate daily, which could wipe out thin margins instantly.
*   **Logistics (Open Question):** The exact cost of last-mile delivery per drop in Pune student areas remains unknown.
*   **Consumer Preference (Open Question):** It is unconfirmed whether students will pay a premium for "home-cooked" quality or if they will always default to the cheapest "mess" option.

---

## 7. Recommended Next Steps

1.  **REVISE PRICING:** You must determine if the ₹2500 is for *one* meal a day or *three*. If it is for three, the price must be significantly increased (likely to ₹6,000–₹9,000/month) to be viable.
2.  **CONDUCT UNIT ECONOMIC MODELING:** Calculate the exact cost of: (Raw Ingredients + Packaging + Delivery Rider Fee + App Transaction Fee + Marketing) per meal.
3.  **NICHING:** Instead of competing with "messes" on price, compete with "Swiggy/Zomato" on *health and consistency* by targeting the premium student segment (those willing to pay ₹150+ per meal).
4.  **PILOT TEST:** Before building an app, run a manual WhatsApp-based subscription service for 10 students to test actual food costs and delivery logistics.
